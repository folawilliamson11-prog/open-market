OpenMarket

An independent enterprise e-commerce concept exploring how customers could discover products, compare reliable sellers and complete purchases through an accessible mobile-first marketplace.

Project overview

A clearer and more trustworthy marketplace experience

OpenMarket was created as a portfolio concept to demonstrate product strategy, customer-centred design, information architecture, interaction design and accessible interface design.

The opportunity

Online shoppers can face overwhelming product choices, inconsistent seller information, unclear delivery expectations and complicated checkout journeys.

The concept investigates how a marketplace could reduce those difficulties without removing the range and flexibility customers expect from e-commerce services.

Design objective

Create a responsive marketplace that helps customers find relevant products, judge seller reliability and complete purchases with confidence.

The challenge

Removing uncertainty from product discovery and checkout

The experience needed to support quick decisions while remaining understandable for customers with different levels of digital confidence.

01

Complex discovery

Customers need useful search, familiar categories and filters that narrow choices without creating additional confusion.

02

Trust concerns

Seller ratings, delivery information, returns and payment protection need to be visible before purchase.

03

Checkout friction

Long forms, unexpected costs and unclear progress can cause customers to abandon their baskets.

Design process

A structured approach from problem to interface

The project follows a practical product-design process. Because this is a concept case study, the research findings and outcomes are presented as design hypotheses rather than verified company results.

Discover

Reviewed common marketplace journeys and identified potential friction around discovery, trust, delivery and payment.

Define

Organised the findings into customer needs, business opportunities and an initial problem statement.

Develop

Created user flows, information architecture, wireframes and reusable interface patterns.

Evaluate

Reviewed the proposed screens against usability, responsiveness and accessibility requirements.

User perspective

Designing for confident decision-making

A provisional persona helped keep the concept focused on customer needs throughout the design process.

Amara

Busy mobile marketplace customer

Primary goal

Find a suitable product from a dependable seller and complete the purchase quickly without hidden costs or confusing steps.

Important needs

  • Clear product and delivery information
  • Useful filters and comparison options
  • Visible seller ratings and customer reviews
  • Secure payment and a simple checkout journey
  • Accessible content across different screen sizes

Interface exploration

Mobile-first OpenMarket screens

The screens demonstrate the visual direction for product discovery, categories, product details, the basket and checkout.

Proposed solution

Three principles guiding the experience

The proposed interface prioritises clarity, trust and a predictable route through the marketplace.

Focused discovery

Clear search, categories and filters help customers reduce large product selections.

Visible trust signals

Seller information, reviews, delivery details and returns are placed close to purchase decisions.

Simplified checkout

A logical sequence and clear order summary reduce uncertainty before payment.

Inclusive design

Accessibility considered from the beginning

The proposed experience uses understandable content, visible focus states and responsive layouts to support a wider range of customers.

  • Meaningful headings and page structure
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation
  • Visible keyboard focus indicators
  • Readable colour contrast
  • Descriptive labels and alternative text
  • Responsive mobile and desktop layouts

Outcome and reflection

A scalable foundation for a marketplace product

What the concept demonstrates

OpenMarket demonstrates how research assumptions, product strategy, information architecture, interaction design and accessible visual design can be brought together in one end-to-end marketplace experience.

The next stage would be moderated usability testing with genuine marketplace customers, followed by design revisions and accessibility testing with assistive technologies.
